## Main answer (latest)

**The historical public-Alpaca-fitted A1 plus public A2 remains extremely strong
when the old inputs and metric are kept fixed.** Its best result was 99.9285%
on both the untouched public target and the Finance-fine-tuned target
(13,980/13,990 tokens and 124/128 complete inputs). On the heavy Vikhr full-SFT
target, the best adaptive configuration reached 99.8213% (13,965/13,990 and
115/128 complete inputs).

The best single accuracy/cost compromise is the frozen adaptive K256-to-K512
configuration with a 0.99 immediate-A1 path and public-fitted normalized-margin
thresholds. It stayed between 99.7999% and 99.8213% across all three targets,
used about 1.63 million candidate simulations, and took 94--116 seconds. The
multistage configuration is the low-cost choice: 99.7641--99.7927% in 49--52
seconds with only 58,068--66,128 simulations.

The old strict suffix-abstaining configuration itself reproduced 98.2202% on
the Finance target but fell to 95.6183% on the heavy target. That is a
configuration-transfer failure in its confidence gate and suffix stopping, not
a collapse of historical A1 candidate generation. A non-abstaining or
recalibrated selector avoids it.

The plain untouched-checkpoint A1 remains inadequate. Even with centered K512
selection it reached only 73.5525%, 74.2030%, and 75.1251% across the three
targets and reconstructed 0/128 complete inputs in every case. The public
Alpaca-fitted mapping is therefore doing essential work; it contains no target
fine-tune information, but it is not equivalent to an unfitted base checkpoint.

R3's 64-input GrandMaster panel remains a useful different-input robustness
test, but it cannot be used to estimate target-only degradation from the
historical 98.22% score. R4 is the comparable target-only bridge.

The study used four conditions, two candidate proposers, and three selection policies:

- canonical clean Pile/LoRA at cut 4;

### What caused the remaining errors

For the best heavy-target control, the true token appeared in the top 512
candidates 78.9672% of the time. When present, A2 selected it correctly 98.0890%
of the time. On the matched public-base target those values were 83.3766% and
99.6324%. Thus most remaining failure is candidate-generation recall, with a
smaller selector degradation after fine-tuning.

The strict untouched-checkpoint proposer was worse than the Alpaca control at
every tested budget on the heavy target. At K512 its candidate recall was
71.2568%, and conditional selector accuracy was 97.3527%. This experiment
therefore justifies retaining and explicitly explaining the public fitted lens;
it does not show that an untouched public checkpoint is sufficient by itself.
It does not establish that Alpaca is the uniquely best public fitting corpus; it
only shows that this historical fitted lens is far better than the tested
zero-fit identity mapping. Alternative public fit corpora require a frozen study.

### Blindness, batch correction, and claim limit

The 64 records were HMAC-selected before target observations were prepared.
Reconstruction ran inside fail-closed user, mount, network, and PID namespaces;
the heavy target weights and truth were absent, the lens was mounted only for
the labeled control, and every prediction was frozen before the selection key or
truth was opened.

An initial batch-8 run could not complete heavy-target K512. Exact comparison of
matched-target batch-8 and batch-4 archives then found 34 different K64 token
predictions, so batch size was not treated as harmless. Every final cell was
rerun or retained from the complete batch-4 rerun, and the freeze gate required
batch 4 for all six prediction archives. All eight failed or excluded attempts
are preserved.

This is one 64-input panel from one heavy SFT target. It supports a
same-prompts target-shift diagnosis, not a universal claim about all fine-tunes,
and it does not replace the mandatory dual-canonical protocol.
The prompts came from the public test split of the target's declared fine-tuning
dataset. The model card does not establish that every test record was excluded
from its training mixture, so this study makes no unseen-text-generalization
claim.

### Claim limit and next test

This panel produced 11 distinct accuracy values and therefore solved the
diagnostic saturation problem. It did not create fresh blind evidence. The next
confirmatory experiment should freeze centered K512, centered K256, the
multistage policy, the RMS-gated K256->512 policy, and direct K256 before a new
hidden Finance target update and record selection exist, then run that panel on
both canonical setups. If centered K512 again equals top-512 recall, remaining
accuracy work must improve the proposer rather than the selector.

## Why old A2 fails

The A1 true-token rank distributions are nearly the same across setups: median rank 1 on both, with K=32 recall of 98.758% new versus 99.035% historical. This rules out the intuitive but incorrect explanation that A1 can no longer generate the tokens.

In the fixed-K A1+A2 diagnostic, every new-benchmark record abstained, at a mean first-abstention position of 1.17 tokens. Historically, 125/128 records eventually abstained, but the mean first abstention was at position 47.74. A2 evaluated only 75 token positions on the new setup versus 6,349 historically. Its normalized winner score was also shifted (all-evaluated mean 1.774 new versus 2.531 historical). The failure is therefore a selector confidence/scale-transfer problem compounded by autoregressive abstention, not an A1 candidate-recall problem.

## Calibrated A2 replacement

Method: `a1_scale_calibrated_adaptive_causal_k32_to64`.

It creates public A1 top-64 candidates, evaluates the first 32 with the causal selector, and computes a scale-normalized top-two gap: `(top1 - top2) / RMS(score deviations)`. It expands to ranks 33–64 only when that gap is at or below the frozen public-development threshold, and it never abstains.

Calibration used only public Pile development text and public base/LoRA updates. The fit conditions were public base, LoRA 2601, and LoRA 2602; LoRA 2603 was held out. No canonical benchmark truth, observation, or target-LoRA input was used. The frozen threshold is `1.2544946670532227`.

The fit split recovered 21 of the 22 gains available from always using K=64 (3,707 correct versus 3,708) while starting at K=32. The untouched public-LoRA holdout scored 1,236/1,248 (99.038%) and caused no revision. The calibration artifact was frozen before blind selection and evaluation.
