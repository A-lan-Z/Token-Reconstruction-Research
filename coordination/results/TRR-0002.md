# TRR-0002 — Controlled hybrid token-reconstruction study

## Disposition

**COMPLETE, UPDATED BY OWNER REVISION R3.** R3 tested the requested
untouched-base-only surrogate and a popular, substantially fine-tuned target.
All 24 frozen cells completed across both canonical setups and the paired
matched/heavy target panel. The negative result is important: the plain
checkpoint proposer did not replace the historical fitted A1 lens, and no method
reconstructed a complete fresh-panel input.

This work follows the repository owner's explicit override and revisions through
`coordination/requests/TRR-0002-OWNER-REVISION-R3.md`. It preserves every
earlier result, does not change the charter, does not merge pull request #3, and
does not start TRR-0003.

## Main answer (latest)

**Using only the untouched public checkpoint is not a competitive substitute for
the historical Alpaca-fitted A1 lens in the tested construction.** Its best
configuration reached 84.46% on canonical clean Pile, 74.20% on historical
Finance, 73.36% on the matched GrandMaster panel, and 69.37% on the heavily
fine-tuned target. It reconstructed zero complete inputs in every setup.

The best tested configuration on the new heavy target retained the historical
public Alpaca lens, used untouched public Llama layers 0--3 for candidate
simulation, always evaluated 512 candidates, compared them after subtracting the
candidate-group mean, used no fast path or adaptive gate, and always emitted the
winner. It recovered 3,285/4,241 tokens (**77.4581%**) and **0/64 complete
inputs** in 362.57 seconds.

On public-target activations from the identical prompts, that same
configuration reached 83.0700%. Heavy fine-tuning therefore caused a **5.6119
percentage-point** loss. The best lower-cost point was direct K256 at 69.1818%
in 57.13 seconds; K512 gained 8.2763 points but took 6.35 times as long as K256
and 15.02 times as long as K64.

These R3 methods are a robustness diagnostic, not new active replacements. The
owner-R1 frozen direct-K256 method remains the official fresh-blind and
dual-canonical accuracy winner.

## Owner-R3 strict-surrogate and heavy-fine-tune study

### Verified target lineage

The official one-billion-parameter Instruct checkpoint is named
`meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct`, revision
`9213176726f574b556790deb65791e0c5aa438b6`; Meta did not publish an official
“Llama-3.1-1B-Instruct” checkpoint. The heavy target was
`Vikhrmodels/Vikhr-Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct`, revision
`7fa9d06a59246629244cdd3b6b92e4fc756baa0f`.

The target model card and configuration both identify the exact Meta checkpoint
as the base. It is a full 1.2358-billion-parameter supervised fine-tune rather
than a LoRA adapter or renamed repack, trained on roughly 150,000 instructions
from `Vikhrmodels/GrandMaster-PRO-MAX`. The base and target tokenizer files are
byte-identical. At the frozen popularity snapshot it had 5,616 downloads in the
prior month and 46 likes. Direct weight comparison found:

- 966,726,183/1,235,814,400 parameter values changed (78.2258%);
- 114 changed tensors;
- all-weight relative L2 distance 0.040147; and
- embedding plus layers 0--3 relative L2 distance 0.021564.

This is therefore a materially fine-tuned derivative of the requested exact
public model family.

### Frozen design

The study used four conditions, two candidate proposers, and three selection policies:

- canonical clean Pile/LoRA at cut 4;
- canonical historical Finance at cut 4;
- 64 selected GrandMaster prompts observed through the untouched public
  checkpoint; and
- the same 64 prompts observed through the heavy Vikhr target.

- **Strict checkpoint proposer:** zero training rows and zero fitted parameters;
  rank the observed layer-4 activation directly against the untouched
  checkpoint's input embeddings.
- **Historical control:** the existing public Alpaca affine lens, SHA-256
  `33b825dff8eb13cfe877a55bb14e3404c4e3f66355e271fb29004b2d49f4a742`.
- **A2 in every cell:** untouched public checkpoint layers 0--3, using only the
  reconstructed prefix and making zero target-prefix calls.
- Fixed direct K64, fixed direct K256, and fixed group-centered K512; no fast
  path, adaptivity, abstention, or fitted gate in any of these three policies.

The heavy panel contained 16 inputs in each token-length bin 16--32, 33--64,
65--96, and 97--128, for 64 inputs and 4,241 scored post-BOS tokens.

The whole-input metric changes the interpretation materially. Even the 77.46%
heavy-target winner made at least one token error in every input: **0/64 token
complete, 0/64 decoded-text complete, and 0/64 exact original source strings**.
Its mean was 14.94 errors per failed input.

### What caused the remaining errors

For the best heavy-target control, the true token appeared in the top 512
candidates 78.9672% of the time. When present, A2 selected it correctly 98.0890%
of the time. On the matched public-base target those values were 83.3766% and
99.6324%. Thus most remaining failure is candidate-generation recall, with a
smaller selector degradation after fine-tuning.

The strict untouched-checkpoint proposer was worse than the Alpaca control at
every tested budget on the heavy target. At K512 its candidate recall was
71.2568%, and conditional selector accuracy was 97.3527%. This experiment
therefore justifies retaining and explicitly explaining the public fitted lens;
it does not show that an untouched public checkpoint is sufficient by itself.
It does not establish that Alpaca is the uniquely best public fitting corpus; it
only shows that this historical fitted lens is far better than the tested
zero-fit identity mapping. Alternative public fit corpora require a frozen study.

### Blindness, batch correction, and claim limit

The 64 records were HMAC-selected before target observations were prepared.
Reconstruction ran inside fail-closed user, mount, network, and PID namespaces;
the heavy target weights and truth were absent, the lens was mounted only for
the labeled control, and every prediction was frozen before the selection key or
truth was opened.

An initial batch-8 run could not complete heavy-target K512. Exact comparison of
matched-target batch-8 and batch-4 archives then found 34 different K64 token
predictions, so batch size was not treated as harmless. Every final cell was
rerun or retained from the complete batch-4 rerun, and the freeze gate required
batch 4 for all six prediction archives. All eight failed or excluded attempts
are preserved.

This is one 64-input panel from one heavy SFT target. It supports a
same-prompts target-shift diagnosis, not a universal claim about all fine-tunes,
and it does not replace the mandatory dual-canonical protocol.
The prompts came from the public test split of the target's declared fine-tuning
dataset. The model card does not establish that every test record was excluded
from its training mixture, so this study makes no unseen-text-generalization
claim.

| 32 | 13,855/13,990 | 99.0350% |

Direct K256 made 12 selector errors even when the true token was present;
centered K256 made one. Direct K512 made ten such selector errors; centered
K512 made zero. Centering subtracts the average simulated candidate activation
before comparison. That removes the candidate group's shared public-to-target
shift, which is irrelevant in a matched/saturated public test but valuable when
the target has been fine-tuned and A2 has not.

The multistage policy is the most promising efficiency result. It accepted A1
immediately on 8,045 tokens and selected at K4 for another 4,242; only 26 tokens
reached K512. It recovered six more tokens than direct K256 while using 98.32%
fewer logical simulations and about 84.9% less measured compute. Its paired
record bootstrap interval versus direct K256 still crosses zero, so this is a
promising retrospective Pareto point rather than a confirmed replacement.

### Claim limit and next test

This panel produced 11 distinct accuracy values and therefore solved the
diagnostic saturation problem. It did not create fresh blind evidence. The next
confirmatory experiment should freeze centered K512, centered K256, the
multistage policy, the RMS-gated K256->512 policy, and direct K256 before a new
hidden Finance target update and record selection exist, then run that panel on
both canonical setups. If centered K512 again equals top-512 recall, remaining
accuracy work must improve the proposer rather than the selector.

## Prior controlled-crossover finding

The historical A1 proposer did **not** collapse on the new benchmark. At K=32, its true-token recall was 98.758% on the new setup and 99.035% historically; at K=64 it was 99.319% and 99.364%, respectively. The old strict method failed mainly because A2's trajectory-specific confidence/abstention behavior did not transfer.

Combining A1 candidates with a causal selector immediately fixed that problem. The K=32 hybrid reached 98.718% on the new setup and 98.928% historically. A public-development-calibrated adaptive K=32→64 version then reached 99.159% on the canonical new setup and 99.214% historically, with a separate fresh-blind result of 98.718%. That calibrated method remains a lower-cost predecessor, but owner revision R1 supersedes it as the accuracy winner.

## Why old A2 fails

The A1 true-token rank distributions are nearly the same across setups: median rank 1 on both, with K=32 recall of 98.758% new versus 99.035% historical. This rules out the intuitive but incorrect explanation that A1 can no longer generate the tokens.

In the fixed-K A1+A2 diagnostic, every new-benchmark record abstained, at a mean first-abstention position of 1.17 tokens. Historically, 125/128 records eventually abstained, but the mean first abstention was at position 47.74. A2 evaluated only 75 token positions on the new setup versus 6,349 historically. Its normalized winner score was also shifted (all-evaluated mean 1.774 new versus 2.531 historical). The failure is therefore a selector confidence/scale-transfer problem compounded by autoregressive abstention, not an A1 candidate-recall problem.

## Calibrated A2 replacement

Method: `a1_scale_calibrated_adaptive_causal_k32_to64`.

It creates public A1 top-64 candidates, evaluates the first 32 with the causal selector, and computes a scale-normalized top-two gap: `(top1 - top2) / RMS(score deviations)`. It expands to ranks 33–64 only when that gap is at or below the frozen public-development threshold, and it never abstains.

Calibration used only public Pile development text and public base/LoRA updates. The fit conditions were public base, LoRA 2601, and LoRA 2602; LoRA 2603 was held out. No canonical benchmark truth, observation, or target-LoRA input was used. The frozen threshold is `1.2544946670532227`.

The fit split recovered 21 of the 22 gains available from always using K=64 (3,707 correct versus 3,708) while starting at K=32. The untouched public-LoRA holdout scored 1,236/1,248 (99.038%) and caused no revision. The calibration artifact was frozen before blind selection and evaluation.
