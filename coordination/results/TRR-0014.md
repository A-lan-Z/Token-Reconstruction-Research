# TRR-0014: prefix-native token-fragment proposals

A promising no-A1 direction is the combination of prefix-derived geometric lookup and tokenizer fragment expansion. It uses only the supplied prefix and its tokenizer; it has no separately trained token-prediction model. This is an exploratory result, not a completed baseline replacement.

## Why this differs from the earlier solvers

1. Derive a residual-write metric from the same prefix's attention output and MLP down matrices.
2. Look up candidates using both its embeddings and an intrinsic MLP-only forward table.
3. Expand those proposed tokens into proper suffix pieces using the existing tokenizer, preserving a fixed512-slot candidate budget.
4. Let the same native prefix verify candidates against H using only its own reconstructed history.

Opened development failures concentrated on word continuations. For example, after a reconstructed partial word, the hidden state ranked a completed word or semantic neighbor instead of the missing suffix. Fragment expansion addresses that token-boundary mismatch. Published detokenization work is consistent with this interpretation; see LITERATURE.md. It does not prove information loss or universal invertibility.

There are zero fitting examples, optimizer steps and additional learned parameters. The two FP32 lookup tables are disposable caches computed from the prefix; they are not another trained checkpoint. Parameter identity/version checks reject ordinary in-place or replacement updates until rebuilt. The tokenizer cache is fixed metadata.

Derived prefix tables occupy about2.12 GB, in addition to the prefix and native verification workspace. Full setup, phase, synchronization, I/O and simulation counts are recorded; historical A1 training cost was not remeasured.

Combined comparison-process peak CUDA allocation 4.897GiB, reservation 5.574GiB. Every method shares synchronized timing boundaries and three byte-identical repeated outputs. The A1 comparator matched native ordered candidates and returned tokens on its qualification fixture.

A 4096-row intrinsic-table build changed BF16 outputs and was excluded.256 rows remain part of the frozen method. The first fragment qualifier stopped at the reserved-memory guard because a diagnostic allocated oversized comparison temporaries; it was preserved, corrected with bounded comparison buffers, and requalified before new capture. No evaluation matrix was shrunk.

## Alternatives preserved

The raw prefix-response dictionary, diagonal/MLP-only weight metrics, per-token residual correction, whole-sequence damped/Anderson reversal, parallel context stripping, a consistency stop rule, and alternate distance/covariance geometries did not close the full quality gap. Their plans, full freezes, scores, resource receipts and raw predictions remain in the experiment directory. R1 metric-only confirmation was 97.7–98.1% on natural text and 82.7–84.9% on identifiers; its later probes are retrospective. The fragment policy was selected on R1 and then frozen before R2 selection.

## Scope and next decision

Do not call the objective a dead end. The evidence supports pursuing token-boundary-aware, prefix-native proposal generation. It does not justify replacing the current baseline yet: inference and cache rebuild costs remain material, the actual recovered-prefix interface has not been exercised, and the canonical dual-benchmark matrix has not been completed.

The next decisive work is to integrate the mechanism with legitimately recovered, changing prefix weights and populate both canonical setups against the frozen baseline. Efficiency work must preserve numerical outputs or be separately evaluated; do not silently compact native candidate batches or fit an auxiliary inverse. This user-requested prefix-only scope is not a permanent charter ban on other methods.
