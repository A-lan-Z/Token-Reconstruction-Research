TASK_ID: TRR-0001
TITLE: Establish a charter-compliant blind reconstruction baseline and cut-depth recoverability map
TASK_TYPE: SUBSTANTIVE_RESEARCH

RESEARCH QUESTION

Under the access and causal conditions in RESEARCH_CHARTER.md, what reconstruction quality and total cost can be achieved by:

1. a direct, positionwise inverse baseline; and


2. a causal candidate-generation-and-simulation baseline using a separately obtained public surrogate and already reconstructed prefix,



when the activation was generated by an unavailable target prefix that differs from the public surrogate?

The experiment must also determine whether the dominant limitation is:

failure to include the true token among frozen candidates;

failure to rank a present true token correctly;

mismatch between the public surrogate and unavailable target prefix;

or error propagation after the first incorrect reconstructed token.


DECISION ENABLED

TRR-0001 should establish the first defensible empirical anchor for the repository and determine the next highest-value direction:

improve candidate proposal;

improve activation matching or candidate scoring;

improve robustness to prefix mismatch;

reduce causal error propagation;

introduce causal adaptation from completed records;

or pursue a different inversion family.


The next direction must be chosen from measured TRR-0001 evidence, not from blind-evaluation answers used during the run.

WHY THIS TASK HAS HIGHEST VALUE NOW

The repository currently contains:

a charter-compliant boundary-observation contract;

create-only observation serialization;

a configurable public Llama-prefix executor;

one pinned public checkpoint;

and a frozen historical strict-BOS comparator.


It does not yet contain a scientific dataset selection, target observations, trained inverse model, complete comparator assets, accepted reconstruction measurements, or a declared quality-cost baseline.

A new method cannot support an improvement claim until a truth-separated baseline and like-for-like cost accounting exist.

NON-AUTHORITATIVE LITERATURE CONTEXT

Recent prompt-inversion work reports strong results under white-box access to preceding layers or grey-box access to public base weights plus unknown LoRA parameters. Those access conditions are not identical to this charter’s prohibition on the particular unavailable live prefix, so their headline results cannot be imported as this project’s baseline. 

A 2026 split-inference study reports materially lower token recovery at a deeper split than at a shallow split, supporting a cut-depth comparison rather than evaluation at one arbitrary boundary. 

A separate 2026 hidden-state inversion study reports candidate-window and token-category effects and compares continuous optimisation with per-step hard projection, while also analysing ground-truth token ranks and truth-derived losses. Such diagnostics may be calculated only after this project’s blind outputs are frozen; they may not guide evaluated reconstruction decisions. 

These papers are hypothesis-generating context only. RESEARCH_CHARTER.md remains authoritative.

HYPOTHESES

Treat the following as testable hypotheses, not assumptions or permanent rules:

H1. Positionwise token recoverability decreases as cut depth increases.

H2. A causal public-surrogate simulation stage improves top-1 accuracy over a direct inverse when the true token is already present in the frozen candidate set.

H3. A target-prefix update unavailable to the reconstructor creates a measurable quality gap relative to a matched-public development control.

H4. A substantial part of sequence-level failure arises from propagation after the first wrong committed token.

H5. True-token rank and conditional ranking accuracy will separate proposal failures from scoring failures sufficiently to identify the next research priority.

TARGET–SURROGATE CONDITION

The primary scientific condition must use an unavailable target prefix rather than giving reconstruction code a callable handle to the prefix that generated the observation.

Preferred temporary construction:

1. Start from the pinned public checkpoint.


2. Create or identify an evaluator-only target-specific update affecting the early prefix, preferably layers 0–3.


3. Derive that update without using blind-evaluation records.


4. Keep the target update and callable target prefix outside the reconstruction process until all evaluated outputs and execution decisions are frozen.


5. Give the reconstructor the permitted observation, metadata, public checkpoint or learned public surrogate, and any legitimately available post-boundary parameters.


6. Do not give it the evaluator-only prefix update.



The target update may be a public-data LoRA adaptation, another justified low-rank update, or a stronger existing local target with auditable provenance. Codex may choose the implementation that best represents a realistic unavailable-prefix mismatch and must record the choice and rationale.

A small matched-public condition may be used during development to verify implementation semantics. It must be labelled as a control and must not be substituted for the unavailable-prefix primary condition.
