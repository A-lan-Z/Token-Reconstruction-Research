## 1. Reuse the existing evidence to explain the result

Start from the actual local rescue commit `ce04a8d`, verifying the complete hash and dependencies. Preserve its source and outputs. Summarize:

- How many natural sequences and first-error events produced the seven wrong tokens? Which later errors followed an earlier wrong commitment?
- Under a correct-prefix diagnostic, was the true token never proposed, proposed but not chosen, or incompatible with the numerical acceptance rule?
- How much time was spent on input differentiation, candidate forwards, vocabulary scoring, cache/commit work, and non-algorithmic overhead? How concentrated was runtime in a few positions?
- Which earlier recommendations were actually implemented? Specifically record the status and evidence for caching, discrete proposal scoring, and damped Gauss–Newton: executed, rejected at qualification, or unattempted.

After-score true-prefix or forced-candidate checks are privileged diagnostics on opened public material, never reconstruction results. Do not insert a true token into the deployed method or use another method's correct prefix in a final evaluation.

If important traces were not retained, allow only the small, bounded diagnostic replay needed to distinguish the relevant hypotheses. Disclose it. Do not regenerate a complete benchmark without a reason.

## 2. Establish a quantitative cost case

Matching the reported 5.20-second comparator requires removing about 77.2% of current total time. If an intervention affects fraction f of runtime and accelerates that part s-fold, its conditional time is:

`22.84 * ((1 - f) + f/s)`.

Use measured fractions, not assumed ones. An optimization of a small component cannot close the entire gap. Conversely, a few expensive failed positions could justify a targeted correction.

Give the comparator equivalent execution conditions and distinguish latency, multi-record throughput, startup and model-prefix maintenance. The old 92-token result is an engineering reference, not a new confirmation panel or a future performance guarantee.

## 3. Choose at most one intervention from the demonstrated failure

### Candidate A: short-block verification before commitment

Consider this only if ambiguous early decisions and subsequent cascades or repeated serial work are a material bottleneck.

Temporarily retain a small number of candidate continuations for a short block, generated by the prefix-only search itself. Score them against the already-observed boundary activations for that block, then commit tokens left-to-right under a frozen rule.

This explicitly extends the former current-position-only experimental scope. It does not permit true future tokens, B1 proposals, a separate language model, or revision of committed tokens. Model-prefix parameters remain fixed within the record. Keep provisional alternatives separate from committed state and isolate their caches.

Use one small fixed block/branch budget. Establish on opened diagnostics whether the useful candidate is available and whether joint discrete scoring distinguishes it. Simply increasing the dimension of an unconstrained continuous optimization is not the same experiment and may let future continuous variables absorb a wrong earlier token.

Batching may improve utilization, but no speedup is assumed; include all added candidate work. Maintain a fair batched comparator where the usage scenario calls for throughput.

### Candidate B: bounded curvature-aware correction

Consider this only if correct-prefix proposal failure or slow local convergence dominates and the previous Gauss–Newton suggestion was not already adequately rejected.

Use a limited Jacobian-product/trust-region correction with actual discrete verification. Count its derivative cost. Do not run a broad optimizer/damping sweep, form a vocabulary-sized collection of full Jacobians, or use low continuous residual as proof of correct tokens.

### Otherwise

If neither intervention has a measured case, conclude the reassessment with no further solver experiment. Fix a demonstrated return/tolerance/cache bug directly if one exists; do not describe a repair as a new inverse theory.
